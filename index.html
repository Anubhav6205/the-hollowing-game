The Hollowing

A first-person horror game in Unity where your microphone is the threat. First place at a game development hackathon.

This is a project page, not a playable demo. The game is a Unity desktop project and needs a microphone, so it is not hosted in the browser. The public repository contains the C# scripts only. Scenes, models, audio and the third-party environment pack are not included.
Source on GitHub Game guide (PDF)

Story

Five years after his sister Lilly was taken by a dark entity, Jonathan gets a voice message from her telling him to stay away. He ignores it, crashes his car near a forest town at dusk and has to continue on foot.

Lilly's soul has been split into five artifacts, and she can hold the demons back for only six minutes. The player has to find all five artifacts in that time without making a sound.

The core idea: your mic is the enemy

The game reads your microphone continuously. A loud sound, such as a shout, a cough or a loud whisper, ends the run. Sensitivity is calibrated on the instructions screen after the main menu, and a mic meter on screen shows loudness in real time.

Gameplay

  • Stealth and chases. Demons hunt the player, with scripted jump scares and chase cutscenes built with Cinemachine. Sprint, a lean-back camera and a flashlight with a draining battery are the player's tools.
  • Objective system. A ringing phone unlocks the six-minute timer and the full mic meter. A direction arrow, which shifts from red to white as you get closer, guides the player to the next artifact.
  • Win and lose. Collect all five artifacts before the timer ends, or lose to a demon or to noise. Winning moves the scene from night to sunrise.
  • 3D positional audio for footsteps, whispers and growls. Headphones are recommended.

Tools and credits

Built in Unity 2020.3 LTS with the Cinemachine package. The game was built with AI assistance, as the game guide discloses: ChatGPT for scripting help and story brainstorming, Sora, ElevenLabs and Hailuo AI for visuals, voice and cutscene material, and Filmora for editing. Game design, integration and testing were done by the author.

The environment comes from a third-party Unity asset pack that is not included in the repository and is not covered by its license. The scripts are MIT licensed.

Known issues

  • Microphone sensitivity varies a lot between devices and can cause false positives. Use a headset and the calibration slider.
  • After dying once and restarting, the navigation arrow may not advance to the second destination until the game is restarted.
  • A few AMD-based systems have reported occasional movement freezes.
